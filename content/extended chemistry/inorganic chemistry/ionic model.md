born-landé equation
$$
U=-\frac{N_{A}Z^{+}Z^{-}e^{2}A}{4\pi\epsilon_{0}r}+\frac{B}{r^{n}}
$$

$$
\frac{dU}{dr}=\frac{N_{A}Z^{+}Z^{-}e^{2}A}{4\pi\epsilon_{0}r^{2}}-\frac{nB}{r_{0}^{n+1}}
$$

$\dfrac{dU}{dr}=0$ when $r=r_{0}$


$$\therefore

\frac{N_{A}Z^{+}Z^{-}e^{2}A}{4\pi\epsilon_{0}r_{0}^{2}}=\frac{nB}{r_{0}^{n+1}}$$

trends in ionic radius:
- radius of ions of same charge:
	- increases down a group 
	- decreases across a period ($Z_{eff}$ decreases)

- anions have larger radii than cations
- radii decrease with increasing oxidation state
- increases with coordination number (number of cations around an anion or the number of anions around the cation), because repulsions between surrounding ions push them apart therefore more room for central ion

solubility:
- maximum charge density and minimum density coulomb's law makes lattice enthalpy highest
- most stable

larger ions paired with larger ions results in less stable lattices (coulomb's law)

sulfates (large group $\ce{SO_{4}^{2-}}$)
- decreases down the group
carbonates (large group $\ce{CO_{3}}^{2-}$)
- decreases down the group

hydroxides (small group $\ce{OH-}$)
- increases down the group

group 1 salts are always soluble because:
- relatively large cations thus weak interaction low lattice enthalpy




to determine if an ionic substance exists, look at the lattice enthalpy

set up equation, apply born-haber cycle
$$
\Delta H_{f}=\Delta H_{atm}+\Delta H_{BE}+IE+EA+LE
$$

greater lattice energy more likely to outweigh other positive terms and make enthalpy of formation less than zero
