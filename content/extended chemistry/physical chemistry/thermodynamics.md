internal energy is represented by $U$
- total energy contained within a system
- sum of kinetic, potential energies (thermal translational, rotational, vibrational)
- as well as chemical bond energy

$$
\Delta U=q+w
$$

where $q$ represents heat flow

assuming that $PV$ work is the only work possible: $w=-P\Delta V$
$$
\Delta U=q-P\Delta V
$$

in a closed vessel, $\Delta V=0$, so $q_{V}=\Delta U$, $U=$ amount of heat transferred from the system to the surroundings or vice versa

at constant pressure, $$
q_{P}=\Delta U+P\Delta V
$$
thus, we define enthalpy as a state function
$$
\begin{gather}
H=U+PV\\\Delta H=\Delta U+\Delta(PV)=\Delta U+P\Delta V
\end{gather}
$$

being a state function, reversing process reverses sign
$$
\Delta H^{\ominus}(A\overset{}\longrightarrow{} B)=-\Delta H^{\ominus}(B\overset{}\longrightarrow{} A)
$$

definition of reaction enthalpy
$$
\Delta_{r}H^{\ominus}=\sum_{\text{products}}\nu H^{\ominus}_{\text{m}}-\sum_{\text{reactants}}\nu H_{\text{m}}^{\ominus}
$$

where coefficients $\nu$ correspond to stoichiometric coefficients in balanced reaction equation

can also be determined in terms of enthalpies of formation
$$
\Delta_{r}H^{\ominus}=\sum_{\text{products}}\nu H^{\ominus}_{\text{f}}-\sum_{\text{reactants}}\nu H_{\text{f}}^{\ominus}
$$

hess' law: at constant temperature, the enthalpies of any indirect combination of reactions sum to the enthalpy of the direct transformation
